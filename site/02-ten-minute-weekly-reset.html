Chapter 2: The Ten-Minute Weekly Reset

Willpower doesn't scale. Rituals do. This chapter gives you one small ritual — ten minutes, once a week — that keeps Downloads and Desktop permanently under control. The full step-by-step lives in the Reset-Day Checklist ; this chapter explains why the ritual is shaped the way it is, so you trust it enough to keep it.

Why weekly, and why ten minutes

Weekly because the mess follows a predictable curve. Under about 50 loose files, triage takes minutes and feels trivial. Past a few hundred, the task stops being "sort files" and becomes "face my life," which is a much harder sell to an ADHD brain. Weekly resets keep you permanently on the easy side of the curve.

Ten minutes because the deadline is the feature. An open-ended cleaning session is a promise you can't keep; a ten-minute timer-shaped task is one you can. When the timer ends, you're allowed to stop even if the job isn't done — whatever remains goes into the catch-all box (see below) and life goes on.

The ritual is not about perfection. It's about never letting the conveyor belt (Chapter 1) get a week's head start.

The shape of the ritual

Every reset is five moves, in order:

  1. Clear Desktop to zero-ish. Every file gets a home: Projects, Reference, Archive, the trash, or the catch-all.
  2. Sweep Downloads by date, oldest first. Old files are the easiest to judge — distance clarifies.
  3. Kill the installers. Every .exe, .dmg, .msi, .zip you no longer need goes. Installers are single-use; see Chapter 5.
  4. Rename the keepers that matter. Five or so files that deserve names you'll understand in a year (Chapter 4). Not all of them. The ones that matter.
  5. Empty catch-all and trash. Close the loop so next week starts clean.

If a decision stalls you for more than a few seconds, use the catch-all box: create one folder named Inbox-unsorted (in your Documents folder, not on Desktop) and toss indecisions there. The rule of the box is absolute — any doubtful file goes in, no guilt — because a decisive "later" beats a perfect "now" every single time. Part of each reset is chipping away at last week's Inbox-unsorted while the timer still has room.

Making it actually happen

The system is only as good as its triggers. Pick the loveliest one:

  • Anchor it to something you already do weekly (Sunday coffee, trash night, the weekly review from second-brain-basics-guide).
  • Put the Decision Card on the Desktop screen or pinned in your editor — sorting while looking at the rules beats memorizing them.
  • Set one recurring calendar event titled "10-min file reset" with the checklist link in the description. One event, forever, no daily nagging.
  • Stack a reward you genuinely like, reserved for reset-day only: a specific playlist, a specific drink, a gym badge game run before anything else.

When you miss a week (you will)

The ritual has no failure state. Miss two weeks? The Rescue Plan (Chapter 6) is the same ritual wearing armor, and it exists for exactly this. The system recovers; you don't have to restart from "perfect." That's the whole point of building something ADHD-friendly instead of merely tidy.
