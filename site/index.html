Taming Downloads and Desktop: An ADHD-Friendly Handbook

Why the two messiest folders on any computer pile up — and a kind, workable way to keep them from swallowing your attention.

Companion guides: this handbook pairs with local-file-organizer-cli (a tool that automates the sorting rules you'll learn here) and second-brain-basics-guide (the bigger picture of keeping your notes and files in one place you trust). You don't need either to use this handbook — it stands on its own.

Who this is for

This is for anyone whose Downloads folder has 4,000 items in it and whose Desktop looks like a crime scene of screenshots. It's written especially for brains with ADHD, which means every rule in here obeys three laws:

  1. One decision at a time. No item should require more than one small choice.
  2. Low friction. If the system takes more than ten seconds per file, you won't keep it.
  3. Forgiveness built in. You will fall off the wagon. The system expects it and absorbs it.

Nothing here requires willpower as a strategy. Willpower is a bad dependency.

How to read this

Read the chapters in order the first time (they're short). After that, the two documents you'll actually live in are:

  • The Reset-Day Checklist — your ten-minute weekly ritual.
  • The Decision Card — one page of triage rules you can keep open while sorting.

Everything else is the "why," which you can revisit later if you want.

One-minute summary

Everything in this handbook compresses to this:

Once a week, for ten minutes: move every file that earned a long-term place into one of three homes (Reference, Projects, Archive); rename the handful that matter; bulk-delete the obvious junk (installers have already done their job); and dump anything uncertain into a single Inbox-unsorted folder instead of agonizing over it.

That's it. The chapters below just make sure you're aiming the ten minutes at the right things.

This is an education manual, not a data-recovery service: when in doubt, don't delete — archive. Files are cheap; the moments you spend worrying about them are not.
